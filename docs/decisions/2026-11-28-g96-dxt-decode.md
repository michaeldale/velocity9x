# GeForce 9500 GT (G96): DXT colours are NVIDIA's own DXT1 decode, exactly

Date: 2026-11-28

Follows [2026-11-28-g96-direct3d.md](2026-11-28-g96-direct3d.md), whose
V9XD32 T19 run on M-Z2T02D (boot 13, 1024x768x32) had Dxt1, Dxt3 and
Dxt5 `mismatch` with channel errors up to 4 against a tolerance of 2.
The GeForce 210 passes the same checks.

## The judge's rule and the card's

V9XD32 decodes its 8x8 DXT textures itself: 565 ends widened by
repeating the top bits, the thirds `(2a + b) / 3` rounded down
(`d32_t19_dxt_texel`). The first and last bad pixels recorded:

| Program | Want | Got |
|---|---|---|
| Dxt1 (both) | `AF6B68` | `B06868` |
| Dxt3 first | `46807A` | `46837A` |
| Dxt3 last | `5FAFA7` | `60B3A7` |
| Dxt5 | `376661` | `386962` |

Green moves away from the exact third by 3 to 4 and red and blue by 1,
in opposite directions for thirds 2 and 3. Modelled offline (scratchpad
`dxt_model.py`, `dxt_nv.py`), these were tried against every texel:

- rounded thirds: at most 1 off the judge's rule; does not give `B06868`.
- thirds on the raw 5/6-bit values, then widened: up to 5 off; does not
  match.
- 8-bit weights 171/85: does not match.
- **NVIDIA's DXT1 decode as rgbcx.h (Rich Geldreich) gives it for
  `bc1_approx_mode` NVidia**: red and blue `((2 v0 + v1) * 22) / 8` on the
  raw 5-bit ends, green `(256 c0 + d / 4 + 128 + 80 d) / 256` on the
  widened ends, `d = c1 - c0`. It gives `B06868` and `60B3A7` exactly for
  the texels whose judge value is `AF6B68` and `5FAFA7`. The other two
  recorded pixels are after the program's alpha, so no single texel has
  their judge value.

On this texture the NVIDIA rule differs from the judge's in 32 of 64
texels, by up to 4; 12 exceed the tolerance.

## The whole image, on the card

V9XD32 now judges a DXT mismatch a second time by the NVIDIA rule
(`d32_t19_dxt_nv`), every pixel, writing `T19<name>Rejudge=nv-decode`
before the second set of keys; `pass-nv-decode` counts as a pass. The
first judging is unchanged, so a card that matched before still passes
there. Run on M-Z2T02D, boot 13, the same driver as the Direct3D record:

| Program | First: mismatches, max error | NVIDIA rule: mismatches, max error |
|---|---|---|
| Dxt1 | 364, 4 | **0, 0** (1,040 pixels) |
| Dxt3 | 101, 4 | 0, 1 |
| Dxt5 | 35, 3 | 0, 2 |

`T19Result=PASS`. The run's one failure is T5, the 16-bit depth
precision check, which the G96's 24-bit Z fails by design.

## Decision

Nothing in the driver changes: the TIC format and the texel data are
right, and the card's texture unit decodes DXT1 colours by NVIDIA's rule.
The GeForce 210 decodes by the exact-thirds rule, so the two Tesla chips
differ here. The rule is from rgbcx's model of NVIDIA hardware, matched
here on one card, not from an NVIDIA document.
