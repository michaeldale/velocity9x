# The MGA-2164W's Direct3D engine: V9XDDP passes where Matrox's HAL does, after placing render targets

Date: 2026-10-10. A8U4I5 (10.0.1.172), boots 386-388, the Millennium II
MGA-2164W (8 MiB) on Velocity9x `matrox` at 800x600x16. Phase 4 of the
[hardware Direct3D plan](../plans/matrox-mga2164w-hardware-3d.md). Evidence
in `docs/probe/a8u4i5-mga2164w-d3d-engine-2026-10-10/`; the comparison is
Matrox's 4.33c HAL on the same card and mode
(`../probe/a8u4i5-mga2164w-matrox-hal-2026-10-10/b383-V9XDD-matrox433c-800x600x16.ini`).

## What runs

`d3d_mga.c` is the engine. The 2164W's hook alone stamps
`V9X_DD_ENGINE_CAP_D3D` (`millennium_hw16.c`), and `d3d_select.c` gives
the `MGA` type an engine only with that cap, so the 2064W keeps software
Direct3D. Boot 386's V9XHW.INI: `Direct3D=hardware-mga2164w`,
`EngineCaps=00000017`, `Direct3DMode=hardware`.

Each triangle is split into trapezoids by `mga_setup.c`, encoded by
`mga_3d.c` (the phase 1-3 encodings) and emitted synchronously: a FIFO
wait before each trapezoid, then an idle wait at the end of the batch. A
timeout quarantines the engine for the mode. No timeout occurred in any
run (`EngineIdleTimeouts=0`, `EngineFifoTimeouts=0`).

What is published is what phases 1-3 measured:
- 16 bpp targets, 16-bit Z with all eight compares.
- 565 and 1555 textures, point sampled.
- Decal, modulate and copy texture blends.
- Source-alpha blending drawn as the trans stipple.

Fog, specular, 4444, bilinear filtering, mipmapping and other blend
factors are refused, so nothing is drawn wrong.

## The first run drew nothing: render targets at DirectDraw's pitch

Boot 386 (`b386-V9XDD-before-target-placement.ini`) failed every D3D pixel
check, against Matrox's 12/12 shapes. The HAL's counters gave the cause:
`M64Policy03=551`, meaning TARGET_SHAPE. V9XDDP renders into a 64x64
offscreen surface, and DirectDraw gives it a 128-byte row. Without ylin,
the drawing engine addresses only the linearizer's pitches, 640 to 2048
pixels (`mga_engine.c`). ylin, software linearisation, is not measured.

The engine now places 3D render targets itself (offscreen 3DDEVICE
surfaces; not the primary or its flip chain). Each one gets the narrowest
linearizer pitch its width fits: `v9x_mga_pitch_for_width`, which is new
and host-tested. Its origin is 512-aligned, which also meets YDSTORG's
32-pixel grid.

Z buffers take the same width-derived pitch, because the engine reads Z
at its target's pitch (depth record). At every display width the family
sets, that pitch is the display's, which is where Z buffers went before.

Matrox's HAL draws into the same 64x64 surface. It may use ylin or place
its targets the same way; that was not determined.

## Colour key on 1555: the alpha bit is not part of the key

With targets placed, `ColorKeyOk` still differed. The probe's texture is
FC1Fh (alpha set) and its key is 7C1Fh. Matrox's HAL keys that texel out;
we drew it, because TEXTRANS's mask was FFFFh. On 1555 the mask is now
7FFFh (`d3d_mga_map.c`, with a host test), and `ColorKeyOk=1` on boot 388.

## Boot 388 against Matrox's HAL

Every `*Ok` key in the first report file was compared (V9XDD.INI; Matrox's
second file was not kept).

Same, and passing:
- Triangles: pixel, reverse, other half both ways, shape, subpixel, and
  `D3DShapesOk=12`.
- Textures: halves 8-256 and R565, clamp, decal, DecalAlpha565, modulate,
  LOD mag and min at 64 and 256.
- Blending and keys: `Alpha1555Ok`, `MipGapOk`, `VtxAlphaCurveOk`.

Ours only:

| --- | --- | --- |
| `D3DBaseTextureOk` | 0 | 1 |
| `D3DTiledTextureOk` | 0 | 1 |
| `D3DTiledNegativeOk` | 0 | 1 |
| `PerspOk` | 0 | 1 |
| `ChainLinOk` | 0 | 1 |
| `TexMatrixCountsOk` | 0 | 1 |

| Key | Matrox | Velocity9x |
| --- | --- | --- |

Specular is refused (`M64Policy07=2`) because no phase measured it. The
2164W evidently has it; it is a candidate for phase 3b.

From the second file, which has no Matrox counterpart:
- **Depth:** `D3DZCompareOk=1`, `D3DZWriteMaskOk=1`, `ZDepthFillOk=1`,
  and `TargetsOk=6` (textured, Z-reject and Z-accept at 320- and 640-wide
  targets).
- **Equal-depth ladder:** every comparison at equal depth is exact. Blue
  for EQUAL, LESSEQUAL, GREATEREQUAL and ALWAYS; red for NEVER, LESS,
  GREATER and NOTEQUAL (`ZEqual1-8Raw`).
- **Texture matrix:** `TexMatrixOk=54`. Every 565 and 1555 cell passes;
  every 4444 cell fails because 4444 is not published and its draws are
  refused (`M64Policy09=219`).
- **Refused, not drawn wrong:** `SpriteOk=0` and `RampOk=0` (4444
  sprites) and `BlendMultiplyOk=0` (DESTCOLOR, `M64Policy04`). Each leaves
  the target's fill, not wrong pixels.
- **Other refusals:** fog draws (`M64Policy06=4`), and two triangles
  setup declined (`M64Policy13=2`).

`D3DZSurfaceHr` reads `0x88760231` (DDERR_UNSUPPORTED) in the first file,
from both drivers, and before the HAL is called. The Rage XL's first
reports show the same. The second file's Z checks do reach the HAL and
pass.

## Half-Life (Direct3D): ladders and HUD missing, then drawn

Half-Life 1.1.1.0, Direct3D (`EngineType` 3; 1 is its software renderer,
2 OpenGL), boots 388-390. Screenshots and HAL snapshots are in the probe
folder.

**Boot 388.** The world drew with textures and no timeouts, but
full-bright. In play (Michael's photo), the HUD and the ladders were
missing.
- `M64Policy04=33449` (BLEND); the last pair was ONE/ONE.
- `M64Policy05=2890` (ALPHA_TEST).

Two causes:

- **Every alpha test was refused.** `vertex_alpha_opaque` is computed only
  on the render interface's path (`d3d_core.c`); the Direct3D draw leaves
  it zero. The Mach64 and Rage II draws compute it from the batch's
  vertices, and `d3d_mga.c` now does too. Half-Life's masked textures
  (ladders, grates) are 1555, so a passing alpha test becomes the
  alpha-bit key.
- **Additive blending has no counterpart on the chip.** On a texture, a
  ONE/ONE draw now keys out black texels and draws the rest opaque.
  SRCALPHA/ONE does the same at the stipple density of its alpha.
  Untextured additive draws are still refused. This is exact for
  black-backed sprites, which is what Half-Life's HUD is; elsewhere a
  colour replaces what it should add to. If another key is needed as well
  (TEXTRANS holds one), the draw is refused.

**Matrox's 4.33c HAL on the same save** (boot 389,
`b389-hl-save-matrox433c.png`) ignores blend modes it does not publish.
The HUD sits in opaque black boxes, and the lightmap pass covers the
world's textures, so only blocky lighting shows. It publishes the same
blend caps as this engine: SRCALPHA/INVSRCALPHA only, and no alpha
compare.

**Boot 390** (`b390-hl-save-velocity9x.png`, the same save): the HUD
draws without boxes, and the ladder and crosshair are present.
- 96,640 primitive calls, no FIFO or idle timeout.
- Still refused: `M64Policy04=38217` (BLEND; the last pair was
  SRCALPHA/ONE, which this build refuses only untextured or with a second
  key) and `M64Policy13=1233` (setup). Which pairs those are, and why
  setup declines those triangles, was not recorded. The world is still
  unlit: its lighting pass is among the refused blends, and which pair it
  uses is not known.

**Boot 391** added a per-pair table of refused blends and setup
refusals by stage to V9X_D3D_DIAGNOSTICS (ABI 2026101001; V9XTRACE
`BlendRefusedKeyN`/`CountN`, `MgaSetupRefused*`). Half-Life c1a1 ran for
43,708 primitive calls with no timeout:
- **The unlit world is the lightmap pass.** All 28,728 blend refusals are
  one pair: `0x00090301`, DESTCOLOR/SRCCOLOR on a texture, Half-Life's 2x
  modulate of the lightmap into the frame. The 2164W's 3D engine never
  reads the destination, so no TEXCTL or DWGCTL setting draws it. It
  stays refused.
- **The missing triangles are refused in the trapezoid split.** All 2,408
  are `MgaSetupRefusedSplit`; none for vertex range, 1/w or the register
  builder. The split refuses a triangle when one of its planes leaves its
  field: a colour step beyond ±256 levels a pixel, a Z step beyond the
  17.15 field, or s, t or q beyond 32 bits at their scales. Which plane,
  and whether these are slivers, is not recorded.

## Lighting through the stipple, and walls that swam

**Lighting.** The lightmap pass, DESTCOLOR/SRCCOLOR, is drawn since boot
392 at the fixed half stipple (DWGCTL.trans 0001), as are DESTCOLOR/ZERO
and ZERO/SRCCOLOR: the frame shows the lightmap at every other pixel
rather than multiplied in. No blend is refused in c1a1 any more; Michael
judged it good enough and better than unlit.

**Wobble.** Textured walls swam as the view moved. The setup gave q
(1/w) the scale where the largest is 1.0 in 16.16. On a receding wall
q's per-pixel step then has few significant bits, and the rounding builds
up across the span. A new host test (`test_mga_setup.c`, random
perspective triangles through the setup and the engine model, against
the exact perspective-correct texel at each pixel's centre) put pixels 3
texels off at 1/w 1..32. Only s/q is drawn, so the setup now scales s, t
and q together by the largest power of two (up to 2^14) that keeps s and
t within a quarter of their fields. A sliver whose steps overflow is set
up again at a sixteenth of the scale. Every pixel then shows the texel
its exact coordinate falls in, at 1/w 1..2, 1..8 and 1..32, with none
refused. Michael: the wobble is resolved (boot 392).

**The perspective bias, corrected.** Scaling was a new claim about the
divider, so MGA2D `/tex` gained the measured ramp with s, t and q all
256 and 1024 times larger (`b391-MGA2D-tex-scaled-q-old-model.txt`). The
card drew the same texels except at pixel 1, where the "1/8 texel" of
the textures record was missing. A bias that vanishes when s and q are
scaled together cannot be added to the quotient. Fitting both rows of
every measured ramp (16 and 32 texels, plain and scaled) gives the card's
rule: an eighth of a texel is added to s and t before the divide by q,
not to the quotient. The steep ramp still differs at the same two pixels
a row. With the model and the setup changed to that rule, every
perspective case passes on the card (`b391-MGA2D-tex-scaled-q-new-model.txt`);
the steep ramp stays a known divergence. Hypotheses killed: a bias on the
quotient (fails the scaled and steep ramps), and a constant in raw s
units (fits s, but row 1's t needs it to scale with the texture size).

## Speed (mwd5)

Half-Life `timedemo mwd5`, Direct3D, 640x480 full screen, three runs
each, run by Michael (`tests\benchmarks\hl1\README.md`):

| HAL | Runs (fps) | Best |
|---|---|---|
| Boot 392: stipple, scaled perspective | 8.016, 8.391, 8.387 | **8.391** |
| + no end-of-batch idle wait, FIFO credit, state-register cache (boot 393) | 7.337, 7.953, 7.952 | 7.953 |
| as boot 393 with the idle wait back (boot 394) | 7.187, 7.773, 7.773 | 7.773 |

The idle wait costs 2.3 % (393 against 394). The cache looked up each of
a trapezoid's ~37 writes three times over 11 offsets to save at most 11
posted writes, and is the suspected cost; it is removed. The build with
no idle wait and FIFO credit but no cache was not measured. Nothing here
is timed per stage: the HAL's TSC buckets are off on the MGA, which can
sit in a 486 with no TSC.

## Not checked

- A 32 bpp desktop. The engine's limits name 16 bpp targets, so the core
  should refuse there; no run has tested that.
- The render interface (OpenGL ICD, Glide). Its draws are explicit and the
  map refuses them, so on this chip they are not drawn, as on the SiS.
- Applications beyond Half-Life: phase 5.
