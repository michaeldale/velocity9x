# 3DMark 2000 on Carrizo: it runs; lines along triangle edges were the sampler outside whole-quad mode

Date: 2026-10-29

Status: **measured, one machine.** M-XP7053's Radeon R7E (`1002:9874`),
boots 29 to 33. The HAL was built on 433b99f2 plus this change; the DRV
and VXD are boot 22's. Captures are in
[`../probe/m-xp7053-carrizo-3dmark2000-2026-10-29/`](../probe/m-xp7053-carrizo-3dmark2000-2026-10-29/).

## Setup (boot 29)

3DMark 2000 needs DirectX 7, and the machine had Windows 98 SE's 6.1.

1. DirectX 8.0 (`DX80ENG.EXE`) was unpacked with `/Q /T:C:\DX8\EX /C`.
2. `DXSETUP.EXE /silent` installed it with no window. The registry then
   read `4.08.00.0400`.
3. Its WININIT.INI carried 53 `NUL=` lines. Windows went through them on
   the `reset` to boot 29 without help, as the Ironlake's did.
4. 3DMark 2000 v1.1 (`3DMark2000v11.exe`, CRC `0FA79279`) was installed
   by its wizard and registered Pro with Futuremark's published key.

The relay moved files at about 29 KB/s: 20 MB took about 12 minutes, and
`put` needs `-ConnectTimeoutSeconds 120`.

## What ran

**Boot 29: the default benchmark ran to its score, 2,860.** Settings were
1024x768, 16-bit colour and textures, 16-bit Z, triple buffer.
- Every draw went to the CP: no refusal counter moved, and V9XHW reads
  `Direct3D=hardware-vi-cp`.
- 3DMark names the device "Velocity9x VBE-generic display". That is the
  name of the vbe INF the ati files were swapped in under; it is not a
  driver fault.

**Boot 30: lines along triangle edges.** Screenshots taken during the run
showed one-pixel lines in a flat colour along triangle edges:
- Game 1's sky had dashed straight lines running across the frame;
- the ground had a grid of short light segments;
- the walls in Game 2 had faint lines.

Ivy Bridge's Game 1 frame
(`../probe/w9qi4e14-ivb-hd4000-3dmark2000-2026-10-21/`) has none of
them. The run scored 2,957.

The mip counters showed `MipGapShape=2410` with `D3dMipChainLevels=1`, so
boot 30 added the offsets to the trace. The "next level" was
`MipGapActual=0x600800` at a 256-byte pitch, where the layout expected
`0xFFDD00` and 128 bytes. That level was not ours: DirectDraw had placed
that chain level by level, after our one-block placement found no room.
- `TexturePlacedBytes` is counted only on our chain path. It reached
  386 MB over 2,866 placements, so most chains are ours, and 3DMark's
  texture manager re-creates them throughout the run.
- Cedar's counter stays at 0 under the same pressure only because its
  level-0 pitch check fails first on a DirectDraw-placed chain. Ours
  passes that check and counts the gap.

**Boot 31: hypothesis 1, clipping cracks. Killed.** The suspicion was
that two triangles sharing an off-screen edge, each clipped on its own,
cut the edge at points a pixel apart. The engine was switched to the PA's
guard band (`clip_in_core 0`, `guard_band_ddi6 1`). V9XD32 still passed
at both depths, including T10's band. 3DMark ran and scored 3,011, but
the lines were unchanged (`boot31-*.png`). The trace's clip counters read
0 in both runs: the core had never been clipping these triangles. The
guard band is kept, because it works; it was not the fix.

**Boot 32: the mip filter off, everything else the same. Clean.** Game 1
had no sky lines and no ground grid (`boot32-mip-filter-off-clean.png`).
So the lines come from level selection.

**The cause.** The sampler takes the level of detail from texcoord
differences across each 2x2 quad. On a triangle's edge, the quad's pixels
outside the triangle are lanes that EXEC leaves off unless the shader
enables whole-quad mode (`s_wqm_b64 exec, exec`). Their texcoords are
then garbage, so the level chosen along every edge is arbitrary. With one
level the choice does not matter. With a chain it drew those edges from
some wrong level. radeonsi enables whole-quad mode for any shader that
samples with an implicit level.

The textured pixel shaders now begin with:
- `s_mov_b64 s[14:15], exec`, keeping the exact mask in two free SGPRs
  after PRIM_MASK in s12;
- `s_wqm_b64 exec, exec`.

They put the mask back with `s_mov_b64 exec, s[14:15]` after the sample,
so kills and exports act only for real pixels. The encodings are pinned
against LLVM's GFX8 tests.

**Boot 33: the fix measured.**
- V9XCZ3D on the fresh boot:
  - `/seams`, a new mode, draws a jittered 4x2 grid of triangles with
    fractional vertices covering the target. It showed `SeamCracks=0`:
    the rasterizer leaves no gaps between shared edges. This rules out
    cracks by measurement, not only by the A/B;
  - `/texture`, `/modulate`, `/mip:1`, `/mip:3` and `/gouraud` all pass
    through the new shader head.
- V9XD32 PASS at 32 and 16 bpp. V9XDDP's mip, trilinear, fog, specular,
  key and 4444 checks are unchanged.
- The 3DMark 2000 default benchmark ran to 3,020. Game 1's sky and ground
  are clean, with mip levels blending into the distance, and Game 2 draws
  correctly (`boot33-*.png`).

## What the evidence says

- **Any sampling shader on GCN needs whole-quad mode once a texture has
  more than one level.** V9XDDP's mip checks passed without it because
  they read the inside of large triangles, where every quad lies wholly
  inside.
- **Under 3DMark's texture churn some chains still fall back to level
  0.** That happens when our one-block placement finds no room in the
  heap. VBE reports 48 MiB here.
