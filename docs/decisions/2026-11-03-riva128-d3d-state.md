# RIVA 128ZX: the Direct3D state, measured case by case

Date: 2026-11-03

Machine: M-7V636C (remote help), RIVA 128ZX 8 MiB, Windows 98 first
edition, boot 18, desktop 800x600x16. Plan: `docs/plans/nvidia-riva128-d3d.md`,
step 2. Files: `docs/probe/m7v636c-riva128-d3d-2026-11-03/state-run1` and
`state-run2`, with `d3dstate_runs.py` (run-length dumps) and
`d3dstate_models.py` (the edge models) beside them.

## The probe

`V9XN32D /d3dstate` (tools\diag\nv3_2d_win32.c). Each case is the step 1
bring-up and one draw into the scratch window. The readback is raw: the
first 128 pixels of 16 lines of colour, then of zeta, written to
`C:\V9XDIAG\N3Dnn.BIN` and judged on the host. Run 1 had cases 1-12. Run 2
added 13 and 14 and repeated 1-12; those twelve files are byte-identical
to run 1's, dither included. No case timed out or trapped.

Three textures:
- **White.**
- **Texel id.** An 8x8 A1R5G5B5 texture whose texel at storage index s (byte
  offset / 2) is red 4·(s & 7), green 4·(s >> 3). Each pixel names the storage
  texel it fetched, whatever the layout.
- **Texel id with alpha.** The same texture with alpha = s & 1.

The quad is (8,0)-(72,16), u and v 0 to 1, as two triangles. The second
triangle's vertex goes in slot 3 with the triple 3, 1, 2 (specular word
00333213h).

## The edge rule

Four models were tested against cases 5, 13 and 14 (`models.txt`):

| Model | +1/2 | +1/4 | +3/4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integer sample points, exact vertices | 114 differ | 94 | 134 |
| Integer sample points, vertices floored | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| Integer sample points, vertices rounded | 150 | 0 | 150 |
| Half-pixel sample points, exact vertices | 0 | 16 | 94 |

Only one model fits all three. **The rasteriser drops the vertex's
fraction, then fills by the top-left rule at integer sample points.**
That is Direct3D's convention for integer vertices, and it explains step 1:
its triangle had integer vertices and drew 420 pixels, which step 1's
counter (written for half-pixel centres) reported as 384 inside and 36
outside. The engine converts x and y to 1/16 pixel (envytools'
`nv03_pgraph_convert_xy`), so the fraction reaches the chip. Coverage does
not use it.

Perspective (case 4) fits integer sample points too: a half-pixel sample
point puts texel 1's boundary at x = 31, not the measured 32.

## What the evidence disputes

- envytools' `nv03_pgraph_convert_z` inverts z (`~tv`). The stored zeta
  is not inverted: 1/4 is 4000h, not BFFFh. Either the inversion is undone
  before the store, or the model is wrong. Either way, the comparisons
  behave in Direct3D's sense, so the HAL passes D3DCMP values through.
- step 1's "36 extra pixels" were the probe's own counter, not the chip.

## What the HAL takes from this

- Texture uploads are swizzled (Morton, u in the even bits).
- Point sampling is interpolator 0. Bilinear is interpolator 2, with u and v
  moved by -0.5 / W and -0.5 / H to put texel centres where Direct3D does.
  That correction is untested.
- Fog: specular alpha inverted (step 1).
- Z: z as given, cleared to FFFFh. Zeta and colour writes are Z_TEST
  when Z is on.
- Alpha test, blend with source alpha, and colour key all exist as Direct3D
  states. The colour key value is the texel in the texture's own format.
- One DMA object for all of video memory at RAMIN 4000h, instance word 400h.
- Vertex x and y could be passed through. Adding 1/2 first would turn the
  floor into rounding, which is closer to Direct3D's exact rule for
  non-integer vertices. Step 5 decides, against a game.

## Not measured

- Z LEQUAL and the other comparisons.
- Z_PERSPECTIVE (CONFIG bit 15).
- The DSTCOLOR and ZERO blend factors.
- FILTER's SPREAD and SIZE_ADJUST (every case wrote 0).
- Mipmapping.
- Wrap modes other than WRAP.
- The bilinear correction above.
- Whether the dither seen in cases 1 and 10 can be turned off. envytools
  reads it from PGRAPH DEBUG_D bit 15 in the Z point object's ROP, but that
  was not tried here.
- Why interpolator 1 makes uneven texels.
